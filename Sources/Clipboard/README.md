# Clipboard history

Copy and paste with `⌘C` / `⌘V` as usual; alfresco only watches the
clipboard and records each copy: text, images (screenshots, "Copy Image" in a
browser, ...) and files copied in Finder. Press **`⌘⌥C`** to open the history:

- Type to filter (case and accents don't matter: `cafe` finds `Café`), `↑` / `↓` to choose, `↩` (or a click) to paste the
  item into the app you were using. It is also put back on the clipboard.
- `⌘1` to `⌘9` do the same for the first nine rows of the (filtered) list,
  without arrowing down. Each of those rows shows its shortcut on the right.
- `⌘↩` (or `⌘`-click) only puts the item back on the clipboard, without pasting.
- `⌘P` pins the selected item, or unpins a pinned one. Pinned items stay at
  the top of the list (the latest pin first) with a pin on their row, and are
  never deleted for their age or by the item limit: they're for snippets you
  paste again and again. Picking one doesn't move it, and copying its content
  again doesn't add a second row. Unpinning puts the item at the top of the
  others with a fresh 24 hours. Up to 100 items can be pinned, 10 of them
  images; past that, `⌘P` beeps.
- `⌘S` names the selected item as a **snippet** (`sig`, `iban`): the search
  field takes the name, `↩` saves it and `esc` cancels. Typing
  `/snip sig` in the launcher then pastes it into the app you were in, as
  picking it here does (see [Snippets](#snippets)). Naming an item pins it;
  saving an empty name removes the name and keeps the pin; unpinning an item
  drops its name. The row shows `snippet: sig`, and searching for the name
  finds it.
- The panel opens on your latest copy, under the pinned items, so `↩` still
  pastes what you just copied. `⌘1` to `⌘9` count from the top, pinned items
  included.
- Pinned items are excluded from Time Machine like the rest of the history
  (see below), so keep anything you can't lose somewhere else too. Running
  a build from before pinning deletes pinned items copied more than 24
  hours ago, for good: that build doesn't know they are pinned.
- `⌘⌫` deletes the selected item, pinned or not. The menu bar icon has
  **Clear Clipboard History**, which deletes everything except pinned items.
- Each row shows the app it was copied from and how long ago. Images show a
  thumbnail, files their icon.
- The right side previews the selected item: the full text, the image, or the
  file (a picture of it for image files, its icon otherwise) with its path.
- Files are kept as references, not copies: picking a file puts it back on the
  clipboard so `⌘V` in Finder copies it again, but a file that has since been
  moved or deleted can't be restored.
- Unpinned items are deleted after **24 hours**. Copying something again, or
  picking it from the history, moves it to the top and restarts its 24 hours.
- History survives quitting and restarting. It is stored, readable only by your
  user, in `~/Library/Application Support/Launcher/clipboard-history.json`,
  with images as PNG files in the `clipboard-images` folder next to it. If the
  history file can't be read, it is renamed to
  `clipboard-history.unreadable.json` (then `-2`, `-3` and so on, never
  replacing an earlier one) and a new history starts. If it can't be moved,
  it is left alone and nothing is saved until the next launch. Set-aside
  files are deleted 24 hours after being set aside.
- The history file and the `clipboard-images` folder are excluded from new
  Time Machine backups. This doesn't remove history already in earlier
  backups or local snapshots (from builds before this setting existed).
- Not recorded: passwords and other items that password managers mark as
  private (the [nspasteboard.org](http://nspasteboard.org) `Concealed` /
  `Transient` / `AutoGenerated` markers, and the older markers some apps still
  use instead), anything copied while an app of `clipboardIgnoredApps` is in
  front (Keychain Access and Passwords by default), whitespace-only text, text larger
  than 256 KB, images over 40 megapixels or larger than 20 MB as PNG, and
  whatever was on the clipboard before the launcher started.
- When a copy holds several kinds at once, one is recorded. Files win, unless
  they are temporary copies of an image that is also on the clipboard (chat
  apps do this), in which case the image is kept. Text wins over an image
  (copying cells in Excel records the text, not a picture of them), unless the
  text is just a web link, as when a browser copies an image with its address.

## Snippets

A pinned item with a name is a snippet, typed from the launcher:

- `/snip` alone lists every snippet under the input, by name, with the
  start of its text (or the image's size, or the file's name).
- `/snip si` narrows the list to the names starting with `si`. Enter pastes
  the snippet with exactly that name, else the only one starting with it
  (highlighted in the list, and the hint reads `↩ Paste sig`). With none, or
  several and no exact name, the hint says so and Enter beeps.
- Pasting needs the same Accessibility permission as the history panel;
  without it the snippet is only put on the clipboard. Like a pick, it keeps
  its place among the pinned items.
- A name is one word of up to 32 characters, stored lowercase: letters
  (accents included), digits, `-`, `_` and `.` are fine, but not spaces, `/`,
  `?`, `#` or `%`. Two items can't share a name: naming a second one takes
  it from the first, which stays pinned.
- The `/snip` keyword is the `snip` workflow in `workflows.json`, pointing at
  `alfresco://snippet/{query}`: rename it there. Any workflow pointing at
  that URL shows the list.
- Names are saved in `clipboard-history.json` with the item. A build from
  before snippets keeps the items but drops their names on its next save.
- Not built (yet): placeholders such as `{date}` or `{clipboard}` inside a
  snippet, and expansion as you type (it would need a key event tap, which
  the hotkeys deliberately avoid).

## Notes

- `⌘⌥C` is global, so while the launcher runs it replaces that shortcut in other
  apps (Finder's "Copy as Pathname", Chrome's and Safari's web inspector).
  Change `Config.clipboardHotKey` if you rely on those.
- Pasting needs the **Accessibility** permission (System Settings > Privacy &
  Security > Accessibility), because it types `⌘V` into the other app. The
  first pick without it shows the system prompt and only copies the item;
  until you allow it, paste with `⌘V` yourself. `build.sh` signs the app
  ad hoc, so macOS may forget the permission after a rebuild: remove the
  launcher from that list and add it again.
- Newer macOS versions may ask once whether the launcher may read what you copy
  in other apps ("paste from other apps"). Allow it, or the history stays
  empty.

## Settings

In `Sources/App/Config.swift` (rebuild after a change):

- `clipboardHotKey`: the history shortcut, `.commandOptionC` (`⌘⌥C`) by default.
  Define your own `HotKey(keyCode:modifiers:display:)` with any `kVK_` key code.
- `clipboardRetention` (24 h), `clipboardMaxItems` (500, pinned items not
  counted), `clipboardMaxPinnedItems` (100), `clipboardMaxPinnedImages` (10),
  `clipboardMaxTextBytes` (256 KB), `clipboardMaxImageBytes` (20 MB),
  `clipboardMaxImagePixels` (40 MP), `clipboardPollInterval` (0.5 s).
- `clipboardIgnoredApps`: bundle IDs of apps whose copies are not recorded.
  Add the apps that hold secrets but don't mark them, such as a terminal or a
  browser whose password manager you use (`osascript -e 'id of app "iTerm"'`
  prints an app's bundle ID). It's best effort: macOS doesn't say which app
  made a copy, so this is the app in front half a second later. A copy from a
  menu bar extra or an autofill popup (which leave the browser in front)
  isn't caught, and neither is one followed at once by `⌘Tab`. The markers
  above are the reliable signal.

## Code

- `ClipboardHistory.swift`: the clip model (`ClipContent`), storage
  (`ClipboardStore`) and the clipboard watcher (`ClipboardMonitor`).
- `ClipboardPanel.swift`: the history panel and its preview.
- `ClipFilter.swift`: the panel's search.
- `ThumbnailLoader.swift`: decodes row thumbnails and preview pictures in the
  background.
- `PasteKeystroke.swift`: posts `⌘V` into the previous app after a pick
  (from the panel, or a snippet from the launcher once it has closed).
- `Snippets.swift`: snippet names (`SnippetName`), the
  `alfresco://snippet/<name>` URL (`SnippetCommand`) and finding one from
  what is typed (`SnippetSearch`), all pure and checked by the self-test.
- `SnippetsView.swift`: the list under the launcher input while `/snip` is
  typed.
